SyncSpeaker · v0.1

Set up two MacBooks as one speaker

About 15 minutes the first time. One MacBook becomes Speaker A (the leader that receives your music), the other becomes Speaker B (the follower). Your iPhone sends the music and, optionally, tells the speakers where you're sitting.

Mac A · leader Mac B · follower iPhone

Before you start

You need

Two MacBooks on macOS 15 or later, both on the same Wi-Fi network.

Place the MacBooks

1.5–3 m apart, lids open, facing where you'll sit. Set both Macs' system volume to the same level.

Install on both Macs

Build the app once, then copy it to each MacBook.

  1. Build the app

    either Mac

    In Terminal, download the project and build it:

    git clone https://github.com/vihAan02/sync-speaker.git
cd sync-speaker
scripts/build-app.sh

    This creates build/SyncSpeaker.app. It needs Node and the Xcode Command Line Tools.

  2. Copy it to Applications on both Macs

    Mac AMac B

    AirDrop or copy SyncSpeaker.app into each Mac's Applications folder.

  3. Open it the first time

    Mac AMac B

    If macOS says the app can't be opened, Control-click it and choose Open, or go to System Settings›Privacy & Security and click Open Anyway.

    A speaker icon appears in the menu bar. SyncSpeaker has no Dock icon.

Turn on Mac A, the leader

Mac A receives the music, keeps time for both speakers, and hosts the remote control page.

  1. Turn on AirPlay Receiver

    Mac A

    System Settings›General›AirDrop & Handoff. Turn on AirPlay Receiver. Set Allow AirPlay for to Current User if your iPhone uses the same Apple Account, otherwise Anyone on the Same Network.

  2. Choose Leader (A)

    Mac A

    Click the speaker icon in the menu bar and select Leader (A).

  3. Allow the permission prompts

    Mac A
    • System Audio Recording: lets SyncSpeaker pick up AirPlay and Music.app audio.
    • Local Network: lets it talk to Mac B and your phone.

    Missed a prompt? Turn it on in System Settings›Privacy & Security›Screen & System Audio Recording, then in the menu select Off and Leader (A) again.

  4. Check the menu

    Mac A
    SyncSpeaker
    OffLeader (A)Follower (B)
    Output: MacBook Pro Speakers · buffer 243 ms
    Capture: 48000 Hz · level −120 dB
    Follower: not connected

    Example only. "Capture: 48000 Hz" means Mac A is ready. "Follower: not connected" is normal until Mac B is on. Other sounds on Mac A are muted while it's a leader; that's expected.

Turn on Mac B, the follower

Mac B finds Mac A on the network by itself and plays in sync with it.

  1. Choose Follower (B)

    Mac B

    Click the speaker icon and select Follower (B). Leave the Leader host field blank. Allow Local Network when asked.

  2. Wait for "connected"

    Mac B
    SyncSpeaker
    OffLeader (A)Follower (B)
    Link: connected to MacBook Pro
    Clock: offset 0.009 ms · jitter 0.007 ms · rtt 3.1 ms
    Playout: locked · err 0.000 ms · buffer 242 ms · underruns 0

    Example only. Within a few seconds the link says connected and playout says locked.

  3. If it stays on "searching for leader…"

    Mac B

    Type Mac A's network name in the Leader host field and press Return, then select Follower (B) again. Find the name on Mac A in System Settings›General›Sharing, under Local hostname, for example MacBook-Pro.local.

Play music

Use whichever is easier. Both come out of both MacBooks.

iPhone

AirPlay from Apple Music

Start a song, tap the AirPlay button in the player, and choose Mac A's name. AirPlay only to Mac A, never to Mac B.

Sound starts about 2 seconds after you press play. AirPlay adds that delay.

any browser

Use the remote

In Safari on your phone, open http://<Mac A's local hostname>:8080. On Mac A you can also click Open Web UI in the menu.

Play, pause, skip, search your library, and pick playlists in Music.app on Mac A. Tip: Share › Add to Home Screen.

Calibrate sync

Each MacBook plays a short chirp while both listen. SyncSpeaker then measures the gap between them and fixes any leftover timing offset. Do this once after placing the Macs, and again if you move them.

  1. Make the room quiet

    room

    Music is ducked automatically during the test. Turn off fans, TVs, and conversations for 3 seconds.

  2. Run it from the remote

    remote

    Open the Speakers tab and tap Calibrate sync. Both Macs ask for Microphone access the first time. Allow it, and run again if the first attempt fails.

  3. Run it a second time to confirm

    remote

    The first run corrects the offset. The second run should report a measured offset under 0.5 ms and roughly the spacing you'd get with a tape measure.

Follow me with iPhone

Optional. The SyncSpeaker iPhone app tells the speakers where you are, so the sound stays centered on you as you move. Without it, set Room›Spatial mode to Off and sit between the Macs.

Needs Xcode. The iPhone app is installed from Xcode (Apps/Phone, see the README).

  1. Open the app and connect

    iPhone

    Allow Camera and Local Network. The app connects to Mac A by itself. If there's more than one leader, tap the name at the top of the Room tab and choose Mac A.

  2. Scan the room

    iPhone

    Sweep the phone slowly over the floor, walls and desks, including both MacBooks. On iPhones with LiDAR a colored mesh shows what's mapped: green floor, blue walls, orange tables. When the meter fills, tap Done, place MacBooks.

  3. Tap each MacBook

    iPhoneMac AMac B

    Point the camera at Mac A and tap it on screen. A virtual MacBook appears on that spot. Then tap Mac B. To move one, tap its marker and tap the right spot. For the most accurate position, hold the phone against the Mac and choose ⋯›Use phone position.

  4. Walk around and listen

    iPhone

    Tap Start listening. Each MacBook shows how far away you are, its delay and level, and whether it's playing left or right. Turn Follow me off and on to hear the difference. To stay in one seat, set the phone down there and turn on Lock spot.

  5. Next time

    iPhone

    The room is saved on the phone. Choose Restore saved room and point at the MacBooks to skip scanning, as long as they haven't moved.

Every day after

Both Macs remember their role and calibration.

Turn on

Open SyncSpeaker on both Macs, in any order. Mac B reconnects on its own. Then AirPlay to Mac A.

To start automatically, add SyncSpeaker in System Settings›General›Login Items on both.

Turn off

Select Off in the menu, or Quit. Mac A's normal sound comes back right away.

Troubleshooting

Mac A doesn't show up in the AirPlay list

Check AirPlay Receiver is on and set to Anyone on the Same Network, and that the iPhone and Mac A share a Wi-Fi network.

AirPlay connects but nothing plays, and the level meter stays empty

Confirm System Audio Recording is allowed for SyncSpeaker, then select Off and Leader (A) again. If it's still silent, run SyncSpeaker.app/Contents/MacOS/SyncSpeaker --capture-test 10 test.wav in Terminal while AirPlaying. It reports the peak level it heard; silence means the audio isn't reaching SyncSpeaker.

"Capture: off" with an error in the menu

The audio permission was denied. Turn it on in Privacy & Security›Screen & System Audio Recording. Rebuilding the app can reset this permission.

Mac B keeps searching for the leader

Allow SyncSpeaker in Privacy & Security›Local Network on both Macs, type Mac A's hostname on Mac B, and if the firewall is on, allow incoming connections for SyncSpeaker on Mac A.

Crackles, dropouts, or underruns going up

In the remote's Speakers tab, under Output, raise Playout latency to 500 ms. A 5 GHz Wi-Fi band or moving closer to the router also helps.

Sound is doubled or echoey

You're probably AirPlaying to both Macs. AirPlay to Mac A only. If it persists, run Calibrate sync twice.

Calibration says "chirps too quiet" or "implausible result"

Turn both Macs' volume up, keep them under 5 m apart with nothing between them, and try again in a quieter moment.

The map in the Room tab looks wrong

In the iPhone app, tap Move MacBooks and tap each Mac again, or New scan to start over. ARKit drifts over long sessions.
